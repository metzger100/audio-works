## Extensive source and physics research

1. Search current and historical Arienne documentation, current variant/lead diagrams and original published measurements with known batch/fixture information. Search comparable true-condenser K47-family constructions, double-diaphragm capacitive sensors, patents with construction details, relevant manufacturer papers, theses and electroacoustic literature. Follow citations and archived revisions; retain date/batch conflicts. Forum/listening/marketing material is a discovery lead, not calibrated data. Do not clone a commercial microphone design.
2. Build a **similarity and transferability matrix**: exact product/variant, capsule construction, electrode/lead topology, diaphragm/active area/gap/tension evidence, mount/headbasket, polarization, pattern, test amplifier and calibration. Similar outer diameter, K47 naming or appearance does not establish electrical/mechanical equivalence. Distinguish bare-capsule measurements from complete-microphone transfer/noise/overload. Deembed only with evidenced amplifier/loading/gain and uncertainty; otherwise retain a system result as a bounded analogy.
3. For front/rear capacitance and correlations, case/cross/lead parasitics, sensitivity, leakage and excess noise, compile direct evidence, analogies, physical derivations, conditions, confidence and conflicting values. Where geometry is actually documented, derive capacitance/compliance/charge relationships and check units; unknown gap/tension/damping must remain uncertain. Cite equation/page/figure and provenance for every estimate. Derive physically feasible coupled scenarios without claiming a production distribution.
4. Research polarization dependence, electrostatic softening, charge/reset behavior, humidity/contamination, dielectric/lead losses, acoustic/mechanical noise and overload. Keep these as separately evidenced terms or hypotheses. A reported bias on a different capsule, a patent embodiment or the 60V mathematical normalization is not an approved K47FRB maximum. A simulated pull-in or noise model cannot establish physical safety or an absolute acoustic limit.
5. Build an assumption table with separate **direct fact**, **analog-derived central scenario**, **retained adverse coverage**, **new supplementary stress**, **unknown** and **not transferable** states. At drafting, front/rear C is nominal 70pF with 45–100pF coverage, sensitivity at the mathematical reference is 20mV/Pa with 5–40mV/Pa coverage, leakage R spans 1GΩ–100TΩ, case C 0.5–10pF and cross C 0–5pF. Check current files. Do not silently narrow these ranges, replace the locked baseline, fabricate likelihoods or call the central scenario measured. New evidence outside them requires additional explicit scenarios and a separately documented revision proposal, not concealed removal of previous failures.
6. Preserve the baseline cohort and add versioned literature-derived scenario records with exact references and transformation equations. Test where plausible combinations change 0019/0017 versus 0023 bias/response/noise order, and where they affect BJT charge, CMOS reset/guard or carrier balance. Freeze sources before each finite analysis. Separate uncertainty from numerical refinement and assess identifiability: which missing parameter actually prevents a conclusion?
7. Search technical/patent knowledge for sensor mechanisms and document transfer limits. Give candidate-linked feature leads to Prompt 11; do not claim that reading a patent establishes its current status or clearance. Keep documentary acoustic bounds distinct from electronics predictions and avoid unsupported system dBA, maximum SPL or polar claims.
