# METZGER100 AUDIO

## Brand concept and artwork guide

> **Precision. Character. Built to last.**

**Working proposal — 5 October 2026.** This revision broadens the identity from METZGER100 MICROPHONES to **METZGER100 AUDIO**, with **M100** as the main visible mark. It is a concrete recommendation for review, rather than a claim that a company or full product range already exists. The current Meridian project is a private DIY microphone in development; preamps, interfaces and other equipment are possible future directions.

This guide defines the proposed brand idea, product architecture, visual identity and artwork use. ## Brand Idea

**Metzger100 Audio** is an independent audio equipment design identity built around technical precision, deliberate sonic choices, craftsmanship and serviceability.

The desired character combines **Art Deco, European studio equipment and modern precision**. It should feel equally credible on a condenser microphone, an analog preamp, an audio interface or future signal-processing equipment.

The central positioning is:

> **Audio equipment with precision and purpose.**

Precision is the shared engineering standard. Character can come from the acoustics, circuitry, controls, materials and intended use. A microphone may have restrained warmth, a preamp may offer deliberate coloration, and an interface may prioritize transparent conversion. Each product should explain its own design choices and demonstrate its performance.

## Brand Values

### Precision

Measurable, justifiable engineering, with relevant measurement conditions stated. For microphones this includes noise and angular response; for preamps, gain, headroom and distortion; for interfaces, conversion, routing, latency and reliability.

### Character

A coherent design identity and intentional sonic behavior. Natural, transparent reproduction and controlled coloration can both belong to the brand when clearly described. Warmth is a product choice, rather than a mandatory property of every device.

### Craft

Thoughtful materials, precise mechanics, readable controls, clear typography and consistent markings. Decoration supports the equipment's use.

### Longevity

Design for repairability, service access and continued usefulness. With digital equipment, hardware life also depends on software support, documentation and compatibility. **Built to last** is a design aim; any specific warranty or support commitment belongs in the product documentation.

### Transparency

Explain technical decisions and publish meaningful evidence as it becomes available. Distinguish development targets from measured results. Document service information and any intentionally open design files with their actual release status and licence.

## Brand Architecture

| Level | Proposed expression | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full brand name | **METZGER100 AUDIO** | Brand introductions, packaging, documentation and discoverability. This is a brand name, not an asserted registered company name. |
| Main visible mark | **M100** | Common identity across all devices; use the existing custom lettering. M100 identifies the brand, rather than a particular microphone model. |
| Scope descriptor | **Studio & recording equipment** | Optional explanatory copy outside the logo. |
| Product category | Microphones / Analog preamps / Audio interfaces / Other audio equipment | Plain functional descriptions used to organize the range when products exist. |
| Product name | **Meridian**, and future names chosen as needed | Identifies the product or a genuinely related product family. |
| Variant or revision | Documented, stable identifiers | Appears separately from the brand name. |

Product category labels describe what a device does. They do not become separate manufacturer names such as “M100 Preamps” or “M100 Interfaces.” Keep the same umbrella identity across the range.

### Current and possible products

| Status | Product expression | Functional descriptor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current development project | **M100 Meridian** | Condenser microphone; reference-oriented design intent |
| Possible future project | **M100 [product name]** | Analog microphone preamp |
| Possible future project | **M100 [product name]** | Audio interface |
| Later expansion | **M100 [product name]** | Use the actual category, such as equalizer or monitor controller |

**Meridian remains the microphone's name.** It does not need to become the name of a complete recording chain. Vesper, Atlas and Regent remain unassigned naming candidates from the earlier concept: Vesper suggests intimacy, Atlas robustness, and Regent a premium flagship. Assign each only when a real project earns that association. No future category is tied to a preset sound, construction method or product tier.

## Model Designations

The public-facing structure is:

> **M100 Meridian**  
> **Condenser microphone**

The product name and functional descriptor provide the hierarchy. A compact hardware marking can use M100 with MERIDIAN beneath or beside it. A preamp or interface can use the same wordmark with its own name and descriptor.

If technical identifiers become useful, examples are `MC-01` for a microphone, `PA-01` for a preamp and `AI-01` for an interface. These are illustrative and unassigned. Document their meaning before use, and record hardware revisions separately. Capsule diameter, channel count, connectivity and polar patterns belong in specifications or an actual variant designation; they should not define the brand-wide naming scheme.

## Brand Voice

Use **precise, calm, competent, restrained and confident** language. State the intended result and back up claims with the relevant evidence.

| Context | Suitable wording |
| --- | --- |
| Brand introduction | “Audio equipment with precision and purpose.” |
| Meridian development | “Designed for low noise, natural detail and smooth off-axis response.” |
| Transparent preamp design intent | “Designed for clean gain, useful headroom and clear control.” |
| Interface design intent | “Designed for reliable recording, clear routing and transparent conversion.” |

The latter three are examples of design intent, not verified specifications for finished products. Avoid presenting the project as an established manufacturer or announcing products that have only been considered.

The visual language combines:

> **Art Deco + European studio equipment + modern precision**

### Applying the identity across equipment

Use the complete primary symbol for introductions, packaging and larger placements. Use the tall badge where the enclosure offers a suitable vertical area. Use the horizontal M100 wordmark on preamp, interface and rack front panels; put the product name and functional information alongside it as separate typography.

Art Deco belongs in the brand mark, badge, divider and restrained mechanical detailing. Control labels, channel numbers, meters and connector legends should prioritize legibility. Category identification should come from plain text and the actual device; the palette is shared across the range, rather than assigning a mandatory colour to each category. Keep the existing mountain and beard as the brand's signature motifs.

On Forest Green, the inverse primary logo, wordmark and lockup use ivory foreground elements so the brand remains visible. Brass and copper accents retain their original colours. On Graphite Black, keep the standard green artwork as specified by the brand direction. Do not automatically switch to inverse artwork merely because a background is dark.

The standard and inverse variants share identical geometry. Their counters, mountain stripe gaps and primary beard mouth are transparent, revealing the substrate. The monogram has a filled green field and an ivory mouth. Preserve those constructions rather than adding background-coloured patches.

### Size, spacing and production

- Preserve the aspect ratio, the margin included in each viewBox and additional breathing room around the artwork. Keep unrelated text and edges outside the logo's canvas margin.
- Use the wordmark for very small digital placements, including the 24–64 px range. At 24 px, the primary peak gaps and badge roof detail cannot all resolve. Allow at least about 128 px height for a clearly detailed monogram and about 256 px width for comfortable reading of the lockup subline. Inspect the final placement at its actual size in the [preview sheet](design/preview.html).
- The monogram is intended for a visible badge height of 20–30 mm. At 24 mm, its main gold lines are approximately 0.37 mm wide and its beard rim is approximately 0.25 mm wide. Match the final dimensions to the engraving, enamel or silkscreen process; the SVGs define flat artwork, rather than machine toolpaths or engraving depth.
- Use SVG masters for production. The PNGs in `design/qc/` are visual checks and contact sheets. Export fresh raster images from the relevant SVG when a web or print workflow requires a raster format.
- Retain the specified colours, line widths, lettering and component spacing within each master. Do not stretch the mark, redraw the zeroes, replace outlined lettering with live text, separate the divider from its lockup, or add gradients, shadows, textures or simulated metallic effects.

For viewBoxes, detailed geometric construction, font provenance and manufacturing dimensions, see the [SVG construction notes](design/README.md).

## First Development Project

### M100 MERIDIAN

**Condenser microphone — reference-oriented design intent**

Meridian introduces the brand's engineering approach through one actual project: low noise, natural detail, restrained warmth, smooth off-axis response and versatility for acoustic instruments, voice and distant choir recording.

These remain development aims. Capsule selection, the finished design and measurements will determine the final specifications. The [Meridian project guide](../meridian/README.md) is authoritative for current targets and decision gates.

Optional project-specific campaign line:

> **One microphone. The whole room.**

Use this only in Meridian or microphone copy. It is not the umbrella brand's slogan.
