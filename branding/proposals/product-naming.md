# M100 — extending the original product families

Revised 5 October 2026 at the owner's request. This extends the original Meridian, Vesper, Atlas and Regent architecture across audio equipment. It supersedes the rejected proposal that assigned different meanings to bare suffixes such as 2-M and restricted Meridian to microphones. The family structure is restored; the equipment identifiers and examples below are a proposed model convention.

## Brand Architecture

**Manufacturer / full brand identity:**  
**METZGER100 AUDIO WORKS**

**Signet / Product Brand:**  
**M100**

**Scope descriptor:**  
**Audio electronics & acoustics**

**Brand positioning:**  
**Audio equipment with precision and purpose.**

### Product Families

The four names describe design intent across the range. A family may contain microphones, preamplifiers, amplifiers, loudspeakers or other audio equipment when the design serves that family's purpose. Membership does not imply that devices must be used together or that every family needs a product in every category. These are design directions; Meridian's microphone remains the current development project.

### M100 MERIDIAN — Reference

Precise, spacious and versatile.

For choirs, ensembles, acoustic instruments, speech, stereo recording and accurate sound reproduction.

Microphones aim to capture natural detail and spatial information. Preamplifiers and amplifiers aim for low noise, useful headroom and controlled distortion. Loudspeakers aim for balanced response, coherent imaging and consistent dispersion. Across the family, the intention is to preserve detail and the character of the source.

> **Open. Precise. Natural.**

### M100 VESPER — Voice

Intimate, smooth and voiced slightly warmer.

For voiceover, vocals, acoustic solo instruments and close, attentive listening.

Microphones and preamplifiers can shape vocal presence and restrained warmth. Amplifiers and loudspeakers can extend that intimate, smooth presentation into reproduction. Each design should explain its voicing; family membership does not prescribe valves, transformers or any other circuit technology.

> **Intimate. Warm. Refined.**

### M100 ATLAS — Performance

Rugged, direct and designed for demanding use.

For live performance, loud instruments, mobile recording, rehearsal and sound reinforcement.

Microphones aim for suitable acoustic overload capability and practical handling. Preamplifiers and amplifiers prioritise headroom, clear operation, appropriate protection and reliable use. Loudspeakers prioritise usable output, controlled coverage and robust construction. The exact performance and durability claims belong to each finished design and its tests.

> **Direct. Controlled. Powerful.**

### M100 REGENT — Signature

The flagship family and brand statement.

For demanding recording and listening systems, with especially refined engineering, construction and operation.

Microphones may offer elaborate acoustic design, multiple patterns and refined mechanics. Electronics may offer carefully developed circuitry, useful control and a distinctive physical finish. Loudspeakers may combine refined drivers, acoustic integration and enclosure construction. Each Regent design should justify its flagship role through meaningful work on the complete product; no single technology or feature is mandatory across the family.

> **Grand. Elegant. Distinctive.**

## Model Designations

The emotional name takes precedence:

> **M100 Meridian**

The full model designation adds an explicitly identified equipment type and its technical configuration:

> **M100 + family name + equipment identifier + technical variant**

Keep the equipment identifier and configuration together as a discreet suffix:

> **M100 Meridian MIC-26-M**

The original microphone variant **26-M** is retained within that suffix:

- **MIC** = microphone.
- **26** = approximate capsule diameter in millimetres.
- **M** = multi-pattern.
- The existing pattern letters remain **C** for cardioid, **O** for omni and **F** for figure-eight.

The owner's **23-M** example therefore becomes **MIC-23-M** under this proposal. The capsule size convention should be documented; capsule housing diameter and active diaphragm diameter are different measurements. An example designation does not select the development microphone's capsule or finished capabilities.

### Equipment identifiers

| Identifier | Equipment type | Meaning of the following number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **MIC** | Microphone | Approximate capsule diameter in mm, preserving the original convention |
| **PRE** | Preamplifier | Number of independent signal channels |
| **AMP** | Amplifier | Number of amplifier channels |
| **SPK** | Loudspeaker | Number of acoustic ways, not the number of physical drivers |

Configuration details follow when they distinguish actual variants. Use **MIC**, **LINE** or **PHONO** after a PRE code for the input function; **PWR**, **INT** or **INST** after an AMP code for power, integrated or instrument amplification; **A** or **P** after a SPK code for active or passive operation. The leading equipment identifier remains present, so a preamplifier can never be confused with a small-capsule microphone.

An equipment identifier fixes the interpretation of its numeric and configuration fields. Future device types receive their own documented identifier before use, such as **EQ** for an equalizer or **DAC** for a digital-to-analogue converter. Their variant fields should be defined when a concrete project needs them. Detailed specifications do not need to fit into the public designation.

### Display and recordkeeping

On the equipment itself, keep the emotional identity prominent:

> **M100**  
> **MERIDIAN**

Put **MIC-26-M**, or the applicable model suffix, discreetly beneath it or on a separate model/serial label. Where space permits, add a plain functional description such as **Condenser microphone**, **Microphone preamplifier** or **Power amplifier**. Use the full model designation in manuals, specifications and ordering information; casual references can use M100 Meridian when the device is already clear.

Reserve each complete designation for one public model/configuration. If two distinct models would receive the same code, add a documented distinguishing configuration token or public generation identifier before release. Keep internal hardware revisions and serial numbers separate. Routine component substitutions do not require a new emotional family name.
