# Broadening M100 beyond microphones

**Naming decision — 5 October 2026.** The owner selected **METZGER100 AUDIO WORKS**. The chosen name is applied to the [brand guide](../README.md), current manufacturer lockups and generated artwork previews. The [microphone-specific identity](../archive/2026-10-04-microphones/README.md) and [earlier AUDIO proposal](../archive/2026-10-05-audio-proposal/README.md) are preserved as history.

## Selected identity

Use **METZGER100 AUDIO WORKS** as the full brand name, **M100** as the principal mark, and **Audio electronics & acoustics** as the optional explanatory descriptor. The owner selected the [engineering-led wording direction](product-wording.md) and asked to extend the original Meridian, Vesper, Atlas and Regent families across the range. The [revised naming proposal](product-naming.md) keeps the emotional names prominent and adds an explicit equipment identifier to technical variants.

Audio Works adds the character of an independent workshop. It keeps the personal identity and existing vector artwork while covering microphones, preamplifiers, amplifiers, loudspeakers, interfaces, equalizers, monitor controllers and other audio equipment. The former MICROPHONES subline constrained the scope; the mountain, beard, lettering, Art Deco structure and five-colour palette already work beyond that category.

## Naming options

| Full brand name | What it communicates | Tradeoff | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **METZGER100 AUDIO** | Broad audio design identity with a direct link to the existing name. | “Audio” is broad; descriptive copy should establish the focus on recording equipment. | Earlier recommendation; retained as an unselected alternative. |
| **METZGER100 AUDIO WORKS** | An independent workshop making audio equipment. | More emphasis on craft; longer in small markings and introductions. | **Selected by the owner.** The workshop character is preferred. |
| **METZGER100 STUDIO EQUIPMENT** | Clearly describes the intended product range. | Long as a logo subline; studio positioning is less accommodating of future location or live equipment. | Best as explanatory language, rather than the full name. |

**M100** is the compact spoken or written identity; retain the full **Metzger100 Audio Works** name on first introductions, document titles and searchable metadata. M100 should be understood as the brand mark, rather than the model number of the first microphone.

A quick search on 5 October 2026 found existing audio products using M100, including the [RIG M100 microphone](https://www.riggaming.com/m100) and [Cambridge Audio's Melomania M100](https://www.cambridgeaudio.com/usa/en/faqs/what-difference-between-a100-and-m100). This supports retaining Metzger100 in the full name for recognition and search context. The search is not a determination of domain or trademark availability; neither has been assessed here.

The [comparison preview](preview.html) marks **AUDIO WORKS** as selected and shows the earlier MICROPHONES and AUDIO sublines alongside the unselected STUDIO EQUIPMENT option. All lockups share the same M100 lettering and divider, with outlined sublines.

## Applied branding edits

| Existing direction | Proposed edit | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| METZGER100 MICROPHONES | **METZGER100 AUDIO WORKS** | Covers every planned equipment category. |
| M100 treated as a product brand beneath the manufacturer | M100 is the common visible identity; Metzger100 Audio Works is its full name | Avoids making M100 look like one microphone model. |
| “Reference sound with character” as the umbrella promise | **“Audio equipment with precision and purpose.”** | Encompasses acoustics, gain, conversion, controls and reliability. |
| Every product is slightly warm | Define sonic behavior per product | Leaves room for a transparent interface and a deliberately voiced preamp. |
| “Precision. Character. Forever.” | **“Precision. Character. Built to last.”** | Connects longevity to serviceability and design choices. “Forever” can remain an alternative if its poetic tone is preferred. |
| Four predefined microphone families and capsule-based variant codes | Extend Meridian / Vesper / Atlas / Regent across categories; propose an explicit equipment identifier before the technical configuration | Preserves the original family character and distinguishes microphones, preamps, amplifiers and loudspeakers. |
| Manufacturer lockup across the full 480-unit subline width | Centre AUDIO WORKS across **460 units**, retaining its 20-unit cap height | Matches the selected comparison artwork and preserves the wordmark and divider geometry. |
| Colour artwork used for every marking | Add **Graphite and Ivory single-colour wordmarks** | Supports one-ink front panels, PCB silkscreen and engraving. |

## Product architecture

The hierarchy stays the same on each device:

> **M100** — brand  
> **MERIDIAN** — Reference family  
> **MIC-23-M** — proposed equipment identifier and the owner's technical-variant example  
> **CONDENSER MICROPHONE** — function

A microphone, preamp, amplifier or loudspeaker can belong to Meridian, Vesper, Atlas or Regent according to its design intent. The revised model proposal uses explicit equipment identifiers such as MIC, PRE, AMP and SPK before the technical configuration, with a plain functional description alongside it. These codes remain proposed.

The original family roles are Reference, Voice, Performance and Signature. They are restored as the intended architecture; actual products beyond Meridian's microphone remain possibilities, not an announced roadmap. The Meridian engineering project and its decision gates are unchanged by this branding update.

## Visual application

Keep the mountain, striped peak, geometric beard, custom M100 lettering, Art Deco divider and existing palette. Their identity remains distinctive on microphone bodies and electronics enclosures.

Use the full emblem on packaging and larger surfaces. Use the tall badge on suitable vertical areas. Prefer the horizontal wordmark on preamp and interface front panels, with readable product names, channel numbers and control labels beside it. A precise sans-serif face can serve functional labels while the outlined serif capitals remain in the brand subline.

The single-colour wordmark keeps the same contours and counters; only the paint changes. The existing full-colour background rule remains: standard artwork on Ivory and Graphite, inverse artwork on Forest Green. The filled monogram keeps its own colour construction.
