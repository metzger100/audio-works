### Diameter, rear acoustics, and choir recording

Larger membranes can support lower system noise, but polar consistency and diffraction often favor smaller diaphragms and smaller surrounding structures. Neumann explicitly distinguishes the usual 32–34 mm capsule housing from an active diaphragm often no larger than approximately 27 mm, and discusses the noise/directivity tradeoff. **M/general technical explanation**, not a specification for any candidate. [Neumann diaphragm comparison](https://www.neumann.com/ko-kr/knowledge-base/neumann-in-the-homestudio/homestudio-academy/what-is-the-difference-between-large-and-small-diaphragm-microphones/).

**I:** At an assumed sound speed of 343 m/s, wavelength is 68.6 mm at 5 kHz, 34.3 mm at 10 kHz, and 17.2 mm at 20 kHz. At 10 kHz, a 21 mm active membrane occupies 0.61 wavelength; a 27 mm membrane occupies 0.79. Their areas differ by about 65%. This illustrates scale only: it does not predict a real capsule's polar diagram or noise. Membrane tension, gap, damping, holes, acoustic path length, grille, and nearby mounting surfaces matter too.

For a broad male choir, the useful question is how response changes at 30°, 60°, and 90°, and how room reflections are colored. A flat on-axis graph cannot answer that. A broad choir or bellows/reeds spread across an accordion makes this especially relevant. Pressure omnis also become directional at high frequencies; “omni” is not a promise of angle-independent 20 kHz response. Free-field and diffuse-field responses differ, so an HF rise may be an intentional room-field balance rather than universally undesirable brightness. [Schoeps diffuse-field explanation](https://schoeps.de/en/knowledge/knowledge-base/technical-basics/diffuse-field-equalization.html).

Rear holes and acoustic cavities are part of the sensor. A holder that seals a rear path can change directionality, LF response, and resonance. For 3U, even body vents are explicitly part of the advertised acoustic interface. This affects later integration but does not justify designing a body before the capsule is measured.

### Single versus dual diaphragm; true condenser versus electret

**I:** A well-designed single-diaphragm cardioid can be highly versatile. Dual diaphragms add pattern options but also introduce diaphragm matching and more acoustic interactions. An electrically synthesized dual-diaphragm omni is not automatically equivalent to a small pressure omni at high frequencies. Pattern count is a capability, not an accuracy rating.

Permanent electret charge is not intrinsically a low-quality architecture. Distinguish a FET-less prepolarized capacitor, such as the documented TSB/JLI medium electret, from a complete small electret containing its own electronics. The former leaves the eventual converter open; the latter already establishes part of the system noise and overload behavior. Externally polarized capsules require a verified voltage limit; increasing polarization is not an unrestricted method of improving sensitivity.

### Noise, sensitivity, and overload: comparable quantities

**I:** Where SNR is referenced to 1 Pa/94 dB SPL and A-weighted, equivalent noise is approximately `94 − SNR` dBA. Thus 80 dB SNR implies 14 dBA, not 7 dBA. A seller's voltage sensitivity includes whatever test amplifier and gain were used unless explicitly de-embedded.

For independent, uncorrelated contributions measured with the same weighting and bandwidth:

`N_total = 10 log10(10^(N_capsule/10) + 10^(N_electronics/10))`.

A hypothetical 5 dBA capsule leaves about 2.67 dBA acoustic-referred electronics noise for a 7 dBA total. Two contributions of 7 dBA produce approximately 10 dBA total. These are arithmetic examples, not candidate measurements. A complete electret already measuring 14 dBA under its stated conditions cannot reach 7 dBA merely by adding quieter downstream electronics under those same conditions.

High sensitivity helps downstream noise and quiet-source recording, but it does not increase acoustic directivity or remove room noise. Noise evaluation must include the spectrum and calibration, not just an integrated marketing number. A-weighted values can hide objectionable low-frequency or narrow-band noise.

Overload figures at 0.5%, 1%, and 3% THD are not interchangeable. An external test amplifier may limit a published SPL figure before the membrane does; conversely, an output pad cannot cure membrane nonlinearity or collapse. The capsule's mechanical/electrostatic limit and its eventual complete-microphone limit both need establishing. RF immunity, balanced output, phantom operation, and common-mode rejection remain later system requirements.

## 6. German procurement and landed-cost estimates

### Rules and calculation assumptions as of 2026-10-04

Domestic/EU dispatch avoids importing the order into Germany from a third country. Use the consumer checkout VAT rate; Mic & Mod's net price is converted below with assumed German 19% VAT. Non-EU parcels require checking what the platform has already collected, customs classification, origin and carrier fees.

**The old blanket “under €150 means no customs duty” assumption is obsolete.** From 1 July 2026 the EU introduced a temporary €3 duty for affected low-value distance-sale consignments up to €150. The Commission clarifies that the item count follows tariff categories, not physical quantity: multiple identical capsules in one category/consignment are modelled here as one €3 charge, not €3 per capsule. Exceptions and declarations matter. A separate proposed handling fee is not added as an established charge. [European Commission guidance, updated 20 July 2026](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/news/guidance-and-legal-text-temporary-flat-fee-low-value-imports-which-will-apply-until-1-july-2028-2026-06-08_en). Germany's normal import-VAT rate remains 19%. [German Finance Ministry explanation](https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/Standardartikel/Themen/Zoll/wegfall-der-150-euro-zollfreigrenze.html).

For a simple non-prepaid import estimate use:

`L = 1.19 × (G + S + D) + F`.

Here **G** is export merchandise in euros, **S** international shipping in euros, **D** customs duty, and **F** an additional gross carrier/administration charge. This is a budgeting approximation, not a customs assessment. Do not charge VAT/duty twice if already collected. For orders above the low-value threshold, use the actual tariff duty rather than automatically €3. Capsule parts and complete electret microphone assemblies may be classified differently; this research did not obtain a binding classification. [Official EU TARIC explanation](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/online-services/online-services-and-databases-customs/eu-customs-tariff-taric_en).

Conversions use the **ECB reference rates dated 2026-10-02**, the preceding business day: €1 = US$1.1225, £0.85033, AUD1.6176. The downloaded [ECB daily file](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/eurofxref/eurofxref-daily.xml) is archived locally with its date. Card/payment spreads are not included. ## 7. Unknowns and characterization of the intended capsule

Use internet documentation and existing independent measurements to reduce uncertainty before purchase. Full knowledge is not a prerequisite if the owner accepts a clearly described risk. An unknown is neither an automatic rejection nor a promise that development can remedy it.

| Uncertainty | How to handle it | Purchase implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact model/revision, orderability and delivered price | Establish a traceable offer and the applicable costs. | Basic procurement information; avoid assuming an unidentified lookalike is equivalent. |
| Contact assignment and safe operating/polarization limits | Seek documented limits; establish a safe method before operation. | Prefer to resolve before buying. If unresolved at purchase, record the risk and do not energize the capsule using guessed limits. |
| Actual capacitance, lead parasitics and sensitivity | Measure on the purchased final-use capsule after a safe interface is established. | Often usable development inputs; eventual circuitry may accommodate variation within practical constraints. This is not a guarantee of any required noise/SPL outcome. |
| Specimen spread and matching | Use producer quality control or matching if an intended stereo pair is required. Characterize only the purchased final-use units. | Do not buy four merely to keep the best one/two. Low price does not override the no-unused-parts rule. |
| Intrinsic noise and mechanical overload | Seek measurements with clear calibration, bias, fixture contribution and THD conditions. | Critical performance risk. Quieter downstream electronics cannot remove an established intrinsic noise floor; changing output gain cannot cure membrane overload. |
| Angular response, resonances and rear acoustic structure | Prefer frequency-resolved angular evidence and documented integration requirements. | Critical acoustic risk. A common electrical EQ cannot generally make differently colored source angles all flat, or restore information lost through directivity. |
| Broad axial voicing | Assess published curves and credible listening reports, then measure the selected capsule. | Some later voicing may be feasible, but it cannot guarantee smooth polars, remove all resonances, or repair distortion. |
| Charge, humidity stability and batch continuity | Look for identified production, reliability documentation and long-term reports. | Residual capsule risk; no general-purpose downstream cure should be assumed. |

After the owner selects and purchases a capsule intended for the finished microphone, characterize that unit to guide its implementation. This is useful development work on the retained component, not a comparison purchasing program:

1. Record exact variant, producer, serial/batch, dimensions, contacts and mounting requirements. Measure accessible capacitance safely and account for the fixture/leads.
2. Establish sensitivity and useful frequency response with documented bias/load and the best calibration available. Record limitations rather than demanding new reference purchases by default.
3. Measure angular response and preserve the specified rear sound path. Inspect noise and overload with suitable existing or borrowed resources where available; do not call an unmeasured target passed.
4. For an intended stereo pair, assess matching across frequency and angle. For a single M100, characterize its actual unit without buying additional specimens.
5. Use recordings of the intended sources to assess balance and practical usefulness. Separate subjective preferences from calibrated performance claims.

Measurement access may limit what can be established. Record that limitation and make the next decision from the available evidence. Do not make purchase of unrelated test parts an implicit requirement. If a fundamental shortfall appears, first consider whether the resulting microphone remains useful for the intended tasks and whether the owner accepts the compromise; replacing the capsule is not the default response.

This amendment authorizes no circuit or mechanical design. Characterization remains the stage after the capsule choice.
